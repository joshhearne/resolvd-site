`v0.11.0` turns Resolvd from a ticket tracker into something an MSP can bill from. Techs track time on tickets, and admins price it with rate cards and contracts. SLAs gain the things that matter at 2 a.m.: on-call rotations, breach webhooks and real holiday calendars. Status pages and maintenance windows are built on Uptime Kuma. Docker installs can now update themselves overnight, and tell everyone what changed when they do.

## Time tracking

Every ticket has a timer. It starts when a tech takes or opens the ticket (both admin-set), pauses on its own when the ticket goes on hold or resolves, and never resumes behind anyone's back. Untouched auto-started timers are discarded. Long-running unlogged time gets a warning.

Logging requires a work note. Notes are **private by default**, and admins decide who may post them publicly. Billable is a separate flag, so a public note never lands on an invoice by accident. The log form works out start, duration or stop from any two of them. On desktop, the timer sits in the ticket header under Follow, with a **Log &amp; &lt;status&gt;** menu that logs and moves the ticket in one step. AI rewrite tidies a work note into plain, past-tense facts without inventing work.

![The ticket timer docked under Follow, with the Log & status menu open](/blog/v0-11-0/ticket-timer-dock.png)

Admins set the billable-hours rules under Admin → Time tracking:
- per-session rounding (5 to 30 minutes, rounding up, to nearest or down)
- per-session and per-ticket minimums
- billable by default
- an overlap guard and a timesheet lock
- whether a rule change re-prices old time or leaves it alone

Logged minutes are never rewritten. The **Time** page has a weekly timesheet for every tech, and each asset page shows the hours spent on it.

## MSP billing

Billing stays hidden until you turn on **MSP mode** (Admin → Branding → Customer companies). Then:

- **Rate cards:** a base rate with after-hours and weekend multipliers, plus overrides per work type, per tech, or both. After-hours is judged against each project's business hours, so holidays price at the weekend rate.
- **Bill types:** hourly, prepaid block, covered and non-billable, each with an external code for your accounting system.
- **Contracts** on customer companies: bill type, rate card, term, block hours per term or per month, external reference.
- **Prepaid blocks** draw down across all of a customer's tickets. Admins get an alert at an admin-set percentage and again when a block is used up.
- **Approvals** (optional): approve billable time by ticket or by entry, in bulk.
- **Utilization report** and MSP export columns: customer, contract, bill type code, rate, coverage, amount.

Pricing runs in integer cents, and the ticket view, export and report agree to the cent.

## SLA follow-ups: on-call, webhooks, holidays

- **On-call rotations.** Members take shifts in turn from a handoff time, so there are no shift rows to maintain. Overrides cover swaps and leave, and a project's rotations beat org-wide ones. Two new escalation actions page or reassign to whoever is on call. Admin → On-call rotations shows who's on now and the next shifts.
- **Breach webhooks.** Admin → SLA policies → Breach notifications posts response and resolve breaches, plus at-risk warnings if you want them, filtered by project and priority:
  - Slack gets Block Kit.
  - Teams gets an Adaptive Card.
  - PagerDuty gets an Events API v2 incident, which resolves when the ticket does.
  - Anything else gets HMAC-signed JSON.

  Each hook has a delivery log and a **Send test** button.
- **Holidays.** Holidays can be a date or a rule ("the fourth Thursday of November"), with observed dates (Saturday to Friday, Sunday to Monday), multi-day spans and early closes. No national set is seeded, so you enter your own. The SLA clock, escalation delays and billing all read the same rules. Each policy's holidays are also a calendar people can subscribe to from Account settings → Calendar.
- **Per-location business hours.** A location can have its own window, which replaces the SLA policy's hours for tickets at that site.
- **SLA card.** It now names tickets: Breached now, Due soon, Paused right now, Late this month. Pause time counts only tickets paused right now.

## Status pages and maintenance windows

Uptime Kuma is now a first-class alert source. Its webhook opens and resolves alerts, and with an API key Resolvd reads Kuma's `/metrics` every 55 seconds for live state, uptime, response time and certificate days. Kuma groups act as **services**: when a primary component is down the service is down, and when any other component is down the service shows a partial outage.

**Status pages** are curated views over those services for everyone signed in to Resolvd:
- a page editor with draggable sections
- a 14-day history bar per service and component
- a list of every outage in the last 14 days, including ones that never became a ticket
- email subscriptions for up/down alerts and page changes

A page can be for everyone or staff only, and limited to members of chosen projects.

**Maintenance windows** cover items or whole services, with plan and "what was done" notes, a linked ticket and the scheduled techs. While a window is open, alerts for its items are recorded but held back from tickets. Windows show on status pages and in each tech's calendar feed.

## Self-updating installs

`docker compose up` now pulls published images from GHCR instead of building on your server. Install the host updater once (`sudo deploy/install-updater.sh`), then pick a channel in Admin → System health → Updates:
- **Production** gets each release.
- **Development** gets the latest `dev` build, nightly.
- **Manual** (the default) installs nothing on its own.

At the update hour, a systemd timer on the host:
1. backs up the database
2. checks out the new build and restarts the stack
3. waits for `/api/version` to report the new commit, and rolls back if it doesn't

It never moves to an older build, and no container gets Docker access.

After an update, everyone gets a **What's new** pop-up. Submitters and Viewers only see the changes that affect them. Admins can preview the next build's changes before it installs.

## Ticket workflow

- **CC followers when filing.** The New Ticket form has a CC / Followers picker, and you can remove yourself from the automatic follower chips.
- **Apply a form to an existing ticket.** Convert an email-created ticket into the right request type, moving it to the form's project if needed. The submitter's title and description are never overwritten.
- **Pick-and-choose custom fields.** **+ Add field** puts any single field on a ticket without a form.
- **External refs advance status.** Add a vendor's ticket ref to an escalated ticket and its external status moves forward on its own. Inbound review lists the waiting tickets a message likely unblocks.

## KB links that land on the right page

**Insert KB link** in the composer searches Trove KB and local articles. You can narrow a link to a PDF page or a search term, so the customer opens the manual on the right paragraph. Local articles can carry a source PDF.

**Target KB links** (AI) turns a rough set of links into exact page-and-phrase links, optionally as numbered steps. The model only suggests pages and phrases. The server then reads the actual document and checks every page and phrase: it corrects a wrong page and drops anything it can't find.

## Access

- **Submitters see their own tickets.** Submitters now see only tickets they filed or follow, plus a dashboard of their own work. Admins and Managers still see everything. Per-project role overrides still apply.
- **View as.** Admins can view Resolvd as any non-admin user from Admin → Users, then release. Sessions are audit-logged, end after 120 minutes, and can't change the user's password, MFA or profile.
- **Default project.** Admins set an organization default under Admin → Branding, and each person can override it under Account settings → Preferences.

## Upgrade notes

- **Images, not builds.** `docker-compose.yml` now runs published images pinned by `RESOLVD_IMAGE_TAG` (default `stable`, first published with this release). To keep building from source, use `scripts/build-with-version.sh` and add `-f docker-compose.build.yml`. Local overrides belong in `.env` or `docker-compose.override.yml`.
- **New optional env vars:** `RESOLVD_IMAGE_TAG`, `RESOLVD_UPDATER_TOKEN` (generated by the updater installer).
- **Time tracking is on by default**, with timers auto-starting when a tech takes a ticket. Change it under Admin → Time tracking. Billing stays hidden until MSP mode (Admin → Branding → Customer companies) is on.
- **Submitter visibility narrows.** Submitters who used to browse a project's tickets now see only their own and the ones they follow. Give someone a per-project role override if they need more.
- **Networking.** `resolvd-nginx` joins a named `resolvd-alert-intake` network so Uptime Kuma can reach the webhook without seeing the backend or Postgres.
- **Schema.** 30 new tables, all `IF NOT EXISTS`. They cover time, billing, on-call, holidays, webhooks, monitored items, status pages, maintenance, calendar feeds and updates. There's no manual migration.
